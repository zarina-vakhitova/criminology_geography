Economic weight and criminology authorship, by country

Choose a country and follow it through twenty-six years. Left to right: its share of the world economy. Bottom to top: its share of authorship in criminology journals. On the dotted line the two are equal, so a path below the line means a country writes less criminology than its economic size would suggest.

2025

The axes rescale

Countries differ enormously in size, so both axes are scaled to the country shown. They always share one scale, which keeps the dotted parity line at forty-five degrees and comparable between countries.

Smoothing

The authorship path uses a three-year average, because a single country can publish a handful of articles in a year and the raw share jumps about. The panel reports the unsmoothed count and share for the selected year.

What is included

The 33 countries with at least 100 articles in the corpus, together 95.9% of all authorship. Economic shares are World Bank GDP at purchasing power parity; corpus countries hold about 96% of world GDP, so the shares here do not sum to 100.
